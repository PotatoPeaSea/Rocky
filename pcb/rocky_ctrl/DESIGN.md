# Rocky control board v1 (`rocky_ctrl`)

This is the integrated controller for the Rocky pentapod (v6). It replaces the v5 module stack: ESP32-DevKitC, BusLinker V3, WAGO hub, LM2596, Pololu D36V50F5, the inline fuse and the battery divider. It is a single ring-shaped board that sits on the logic deck around the Raspberry Pi 5.

| | |
|---|---|
| Outline | Ø156 mm ring (r 78) with a Pi 5 cut-out (x −53..40, y −31..30), a Pi USB-C plug notch (x −47..−31, y −50..−31) and a battery-lead notch over deck hole B (x −8..8, y < −60) |
| Stack-up | 4 layers, 1.6 mm, 1 oz outer / 1 oz inner (JLC "1 oz inner" option). L1 signals + pours, L2 GND plane, L3 VSERVO plane, L4 signals + GND pour |
| MCU | ESP32-WROOM-32E-N8 (classic ESP32: BT Classic for Bluepad32, v5 firmware ports directly) |
| Servo I/O | 2 half-duplex UART buses (bus A = UART1: legs 1-3; bus B = UART2: legs 4-5), 5 × Molex SPOX 5267-03A leg connectors |
| Power | XT60 in → 20 A ATM blade fuse → P-FET reverse-polarity protection → (a) ESP32-switched, soft-started servo rail with 1 mΩ / INA180 current sense and 2 × 1000 µF, (b) 5 V / 5 A buck for the Pi 5 (TPS54560B), (c) 3.3 V / 2 A buck (AP63203, diode-OR battery / USB) |
| Software | KiCad 9.0 (files are KiCad 9 format; KiCad 8 cannot open them) |

Everything in the project (schematic, board, custom symbols, 3D models) is generated by the scripts in `tools/`, and the generators are the source of truth. Edit `tools/design.py` (parts and nets) or `tools/placement.py` (positions), then rebuild (see the end of this file). You can also edit the board by hand in KiCad from here on: nothing depends on the scripts once the files exist.

## 2. Research findings: HX-30HM servo and Hiwonder bus

These were open items in v5. Confidence: **H** = published spec, **M** = consistent across Hiwonder sources but not stated for the HX-30HM specifically, **L** = inferred; measure before relying on it.

| Item | Finding | Conf. | Source |

| Stall / no-load current | 3 A stall, 100 mA no-load | H | same |

**Recommended bring-up test (5 min):** connect a single servo to leg 1, keep VIO at 3.3 V and run `SERVO_ID_READ` at 1 Mbps. If replies are flaky over the 20 cm + leg cable, bridge JP1 to 5 V.

### Why two UART buses
At 1 Mbps a Hiwonder move command is 10 bytes (100 µs) and a position read is 6 bytes out plus 8 back, plus about 50-100 µs turnaround (≈ 250-300 µs). A full cycle on one bus (15 writes + 15 reads) takes about 6 ms, so roughly 150 Hz. Splitting legs 1-3 (IDs 1-9) on UART1 and legs 4-5 (IDs 10-15) on UART2 roughly halves that, giving a 250-300 Hz control loop. It also isolates a shorted or unplugged leg cable to one bus. The ESP32 has only three UARTs and UART0 is the console/Pi link, so two buses is the maximum. Use ESP-IDF `UART_MODE_RS485_HALF_DUPLEX` with RTS on IO4 and IO27: the driver raises RTS during TX, which enables the '126 driver and tri-states the '125 receiver. That suppresses the echo, and the 10 k pull-up on the RX pin keeps it idle.

## 3. Power and current budget

| Load | Typical | Peak | Worst case / design limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 × HX-30HM | 0.1 A each idle (1.5 A); about 5-10 A total walking (estimate) | 15-20 A for brief bursts (many joints accelerating) | 45 A if all stall. This is not sustainable: the 20 A fuse and firmware current limiting (INA180) bound it. |
| Raspberry Pi 5 + cooler (5 V) | 1.5-3 A | 5 A | 5 V × 5 A / 0.88 / 9.6 V ≈ **3.0 A from the pack** |
| ESP32 + CH340 + logic (3.3 V) | 0.1-0.25 A | 0.5 A (BT TX) | about 0.2 A from the pack |
| **Total through F1** | 7-13 A | ≈ 20 A | **20 A fuse** (ATM mini blade; 200 % is held for 0.15-5 s, so stall bursts pass and sustained overload opens) |

- **Fuse:** 20 A is the v5 value and matches 14 AWG pack wiring and the XT60 (30 A continuous). A 25 A blade fits the same holder if 20 A proves nuisance-prone. The fuse protects the wiring; firmware should limit servo current: trip `SERVO_EN` low at a sustained ISERVO > 15 A.
- **Copper (IPC-2221, external, 1 oz, ΔT ≈ 20 °C):** about 3.7 A per mm of width.
  - **XT60 → fuse → Q1:** 4 mm tracks duplicated on F.Cu and B.Cu inside pours roughly 8 mm wide on both layers, so ≥ 25 A continuous. Path length is under 25 mm.
  - **Q1 → Q2 (VBAT_P):** F.Cu pour (3 mm) plus 2.4 mm F/B tracks plus a 5 mm B.Cu pour joined by 4 vias. About 20 A, and only 10 mm long.
  - **Q2 → shunt (VSERVO_SW):** about 4 mm F.Cu pour over 5 mm. About 15 A continuous, 20 A transient. This is the weakest section; see the risks.
  - **Shunt → servo plane:** 9 × 0.8/0.4 mm vias (≈ 1.5-2 A each) into the L3 VSERVO plane. The plane is a 17-40 mm wide 1 oz ring, about 2 mΩ to the farthest leg along the two parallel paths, so under 30 mV drop at 10 A.
  - **Leg connectors:** THT pins tie directly into the VSERVO plane, with GND to the L2 plane and both outer pours.
- **Per leg:** up to 9 A stall (3 servos), but each SPOX/5264 contact is rated about 3 A. That is the same limit as the servo's own chain connector, so the board is not the bottleneck. Normal walking is 1-3 A per leg.
- **Bulk capacitance (servo rail):** 2 × 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR (Panasonic FR 12.5×20; about 20 mΩ and 2.2 A ripple each) plus 22 µF ceramic at every leg connector and 10 µF at the switch.
  - **Sizing:** the battery lead plus loop key is about 250 mm of 14 AWG pair, roughly 0.3-0.5 µH and 20-30 mΩ. For a 10 A load step and ≤ 0.5 V droop the rail needs Z ≤ 50 mΩ, and C ≥ L/Z² ≈ 0.5 µH / (0.05 Ω)² ≈ 200 µF to damp the cable LC. 2000 µF gives a 10× margin and absorbs regenerated energy.
  - **Regenerated energy:** when the rail is switched off, back-driven servos push current through Q2's body diode into VBAT_P and on into the battery, because Q1 is a plain P-FET (not an ideal diode) and conducts both ways. With the battery unplugged, 2000 µF from 12.6 V to 16 V absorbs 0.1 J, and D1 clamps above that.
- **Soft start:** Q2's gate ramps through 33 kΩ into 220 nF of Miller capacitance, about 1.2 V/ms. Charging 2 mF therefore draws ≈ 2.5 A of inrush for about 10 ms instead of a spark. Turn-off takes about 20-30 ms (100 k gate pull-up), which is fine for an e-stop.
- **5 V buck (TPS54560B):** 4.5-60 V in, 5 A, external Schottky. Settings: 500 kHz (RT 191 k), Vout = 0.8 × (1 + 53.6/10) = 5.09 V, L = 4.7 µH SRP1245A (Isat 21 A), Cout 4 × 22 µF, type-II compensation 16 k / 3.9 nF / 39 pF (crossover ≈ 40 kHz), EN UVLO ≈ 7.9 V on and 7.6 V off.
  - About 88 % efficient at 5 A, with ≈ 1.2 W in D2.
  - **Verify the compensation in TI WEBENCH or with a load step.**
- **Pi 5 power path:** the board drives the Pi's own USB-C input (through its protection and PMIC) with a short USB-C plug pigtail from J2 (JST VH-3: 5 V / GND / CC). J2 pin 3 presents Rp = 10 k (3 A Type-C advertisement) if the pigtail has a CC wire. There is no USB-PD, so the Pi sees a non-5 A supply.
  - Put `usb_max_current_enable=1` in `/boot/firmware/config.txt`, or `PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000` in the EEPROM (`rpi-eeprom-config`). Otherwise the Pi limits its USB ports to 600 mA and warns at boot.
  - Feeding the GPIO 5 V pins was rejected: it bypasses the Pi's input protection, and a 2.54 mm ribbon cannot carry 5 A.
  - The 2×5 ribbon carries no 5 V.
  - The ESP32 can cut the Pi supply (IO33 → Q4 pulls EN low; the default is Pi on).
- **3.3 V (AP63203, 2 A):** fed from VBAT_P or USB VBUS through two SS34 diodes. The ESP32 can therefore be flashed or debugged from USB alone (servo rail and Pi off), and the logic stays up if the Pi buck is disabled.

## 6. Mechanical placement (robot model frame)

The frame is the v6 model frame: the origin is the body centre on the hip-servo plane, +z is up, leg 1 is on +y, and angles are CCW from +x.

- **Board origin:** the board centre is at robot (0, 0). The KiCad file uses (150, 150) mm for that point; the board X axis is robot +x and KiCad +Y is robot −y.
- **STEP origin:** `rocky_ctrl.step` (and `C:\Users\smart\Documents\Pentapod\hardware\rocky_control_pcb.step`) is exported with `--user-origin 150x150mm`. **STEP x, y = robot x, y**, and the **board underside (laminate bottom) is at STEP z = 0**; the top is at z ≈ 1.6.

## 8. Firmware notes

- UART1: `uart_set_pin(UART_NUM_1, 17, 16, 4, -1)`, then `uart_set_mode(UART_NUM_1, UART_MODE_RS485_HALF_DUPLEX)`, at 1,000,000 baud. UART2 is the same with pins 25/26/27.
- Turn the servo rail on with `SERVO_EN` (IO23) high **after** checking VBAT_SENSE. Then wait about 30 ms for the soft start and confirm VSRV_SENSE ≈ VBAT.
- Current guard: ISERVO (IO35) gives 100 mV/A. Trip at > 15 A for 200 ms or > 22 A for 20 ms, then drop SERVO_EN.
- Battery: 3S cut-off at 9.6 V (3.2 V/cell). Warn at 10.5 V. The Pi buck's UVLO (≈ 7.9 V) is only a last resort.
- Pi-side flashing: from the Pi, `esptool.py --port /dev/ttyAMA0 --before no_reset --after no_reset` after driving GPIO27 low and pulsing GPIO17 high (the 2N7002s invert, so a Pi GPIO high pulls the ESP pin low). Unplug the board's USB-C first, because USB wins the RX mux.
- The Pi's `config.txt` needs `enable_uart=1`, `dtparam=uart0=on` (GPIO14/15) and `usb_max_current_enable=1`.

## 9. Open risks and assumptions

1. **Servo pin order, logic level and baud are not measured.** Pin order comes from the Hiwonder LX family docs, and logic level from Hiwonder's 3.3 V wiring examples. Verify with one servo before plugging in a leg (see §2). If the HX-30HM port turns out to be SIG, VIN, GND, the board needs adapter leads, because the header footprint is symmetric and the pins would have to be swapped in `design.py`.
2. **Per-leg current through 2.5 mm SPOX contacts** (about 3 A each) is below a full-leg stall (9 A). This is inherent to the servo's chain connector. Firmware current limiting is the mitigation.
3. **VSERVO_SW section (Q2 → shunt)** is about 4 mm of single-layer copper. It is fine for 20 A transients and about 15 A continuous. If long stalls are expected, solder-tin the exposed area, or move to 2 oz outer copper (JLC option) without changing the layout.
4. **TPS54560B compensation** was hand-calculated for 4 × 22 µF (with derating). Verify with a 1-4 A load step on the bench, or in WEBENCH, before connecting the Pi.
5. **Simplified 3D models:** the XT60PW, VH, SPOX, HRO USB-C, SRP1245A, the tact switch and the 12.5/8 mm capacitors use simplified envelope models generated by `tools/make_3d.py`, because the Debian KiCad 3D package lacks them. Outer dimensions match the datasheets to about 0.5 mm.
6. **LCSC part numbers** are incomplete (see §7). Some parts (BSC030P03NS3, TPS54560B, SN74LV1T126, Keystone 3568, B540C, SRP1245A) may be JLC "extended" or need global sourcing. Equivalents that drop in with the same footprint:
   - any −30 V ≤ 4 mΩ P-FET in PG-TDSON-8 / PowerPAK SO-8 (e.g. Vishay SiRA99DP, AOS AONS21321)
   - any 4.7 µH ≥ 8 A 12×12 inductor
   - SS54 / B560C for D2
7. **Pi USB-C pigtail:** this is a bought cable. Confirm it fits the notch envelope (x −47..−31, y −50..−31, z 59..68).
8. **The ESP32 antenna** is close to the M3 tie-rod/magnet at post P5 (51.7, 71.2), about 50 mm away. BT range inside the plastic dome should be fine. If it is not, the ESP32-WROOM-32UE (U.FL) footprint variant is pin-compatible.
9. **Schematic:** the schematic is generated and label-based (functional blocks on one A1 sheet, nets joined by labels rather than drawn wires). It is electrically complete and ERC-clean, but less pretty than a hand-drawn one.

## 11. Regenerating

KiCad 9 Python (pcbnew) and a JDK for Freerouting are needed. From `pcb/rocky_ctrl/`:

```
python3 tools/gen_sch.py                                   # schematic + custom symbols
kicad-cli sch export netlist --format kicadsexpr -o /tmp/rocky.net rocky_ctrl.kicad_sch
python3 tools/gen_pcb.py /tmp/rocky.net                    # placed board, outline, holes, keep-outs
python3 tools/power_route.py                               # hand power copper + GND/VSERVO fan-out
tools/route.sh                                             # Freerouting 1.9 (signals only)
python3 tools/finish_pcb.py /tmp/rocky.ses                 # import routes, pours, stitching, fill
python3 tools/island_vias.py                               # stitch any GND pour island without a via
tools/export.sh                                            # ERC/DRC, STEP, Gerbers, BOM, CPL, PDF, renders
```

`tools/make_3d.py` (CadQuery) regenerates `3d/*.step`.
